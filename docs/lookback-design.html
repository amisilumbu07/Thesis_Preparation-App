Lookback

A study app for defending the thesis Time Series Load Forecasting using Transformers. It turns the thesis into concepts you can recall, explain, defend under questioning and extend, each one anchored to a thesis section and a source paper.

1Purpose and what mastery means

The thesis makes three load-bearing claims: PatchTST is the most accurate and most VRAM-efficient model on WTH, ETTh1 and London Smart Meter data; every Transformer collapsed to NaN loss on the STEEL dataset because of scale heterogeneity and on/off discontinuities; and Transformers buy about 25–28% lower error at the price of far more parameters, memory and training time than RNNs. A committee will test each claim from three directions: do you understand the mechanism, is the evidence consistent, and does it generalize.

Lookback exists so that, on defense day, you can answer all three directions for every claim without notes. Rereading the thesis does not produce that. Retrieval practice, explaining in your own words, and being questioned by an adversary do, so the app is organized around those three activities rather than around chapters.

Definition of done. You are defense-ready when every concept in the atlas sits at level 3 (Defend) or higher, every item in the vulnerability register is marked fixed or has a rehearsed answer, and you have completed two full mock vivas scoring at least 80% on the committee rubric.

2Two study modes

Intensive and relaxed sessions draw on the same content and the same progress record. They differ in pace, how much you produce versus receive, and how hard the app pushes back.

5Soundness court: the STEEL case file

The STEEL failure is the thesis's most original finding and its most exposed one. The app treats it as a case you argue: claim, evidence, the strongest objection, and the diagnostic that would settle it. This section is also the answer to task 2 in your brief.

The claim as written

Variances spanning 10⁻¹ to 10⁶ overwhelmed StandardScaler; softmax exponentiation eQKT then exceeded the float32 limit of about 3.4×10³⁸, producing NaN; and on/off step transitions violated the continuity that self-attention assumes.

Verdict: the conclusion is defensible, the mechanism as stated is vulnerable

Objection 1: the scaler should have removed the scale gap. StandardScaler standardizes each column separately. After fitting, a feature with variance 10⁶ and one with variance 10⁻¹ both have unit variance on the training split. A cross-feature variance disparity is therefore not what reaches the attention layer. What can survive is within-feature trouble: a column that is constant in the training split (zero variance), or test-split values far outside the training range.

Objection 2: softmax overflow is guarded in standard libraries. PyTorch's softmax subtracts the row maximum before exponentiating, so ex never sees a huge positive x. Overflow is far more likely upstream: float16 mixed precision (max ≈ 65,504, reached by e11.1), unbounded growth of attention logits during training, or exploding gradients at the model's learning rate.

Objection 3: PatchTST applies RevIN per channel. Channel independence plus instance normalization means PatchTST sees each channel scaled on its own. If it still produced NaN, the cause is probably a near-constant window (machine off, all zeros) where the instance standard deviation approaches zero, or NaNs already present in the input tensor.

What the literature does support. Attention-logit growth is a documented source of Transformer training instability, which is why large models add normalization to queries and keys (Dehghani et al. 2023; Wortsman et al. 2023). Distribution shift between windows is the documented motivation for RevIN (Kim et al. 2022). Continuity and smoothness as a prerequisite for attention is closer to your interpretation than an established result; frame it as a hypothesis consistent with the evidence, not a proven mechanism.

The diagnostic the app asks you to rehearse

If you still have the code, running these checks turns the claim from speculation into evidence. If you do not, rehearsing them lets you say exactly how you would confirm it.

  • Assert no NaN or inf in the tensor after scaling, before the model. Count columns with zero training variance.
  • Log the maximum absolute attention logit and the gradient norm for the first 50 steps.
  • Rerun with float32 only (no autocast), gradient clipping at 1.0, and learning rate 1e-5.
  • Replace StandardScaler with RobustScaler or log1p on heavy-tailed columns; drop constant columns.
  • Wrap one epoch in torch.autograd.set_detect_anomaly(True) to locate the first NaN-producing operation.

Which checks fail tells you which of the four hypotheses holds: bad input, fp16 overflow, logit growth, or gradient explosion. A committee will respect a crisp diagnostic plan more than a confident mechanism.

6The stability argument: RevIN, patching, CI and distilling

This is task 4 in your brief, turned into a concept thread. The app teaches it as one chain of reasoning, so that "predictive stability" in the thesis has a clear definition and each component has a precise job.

ComponentInstability it addressesHow it worksLimit to acknowledge
RevIN
Kim et al. 2022		Distribution shift: the mean and scale of the lookback differ from training data and from the horizonSubtracts each window's mean and divides by its std (with learnable affine), then restores those statistics on the outputAssumes the horizon shares the lookback's statistics; Liu et al. 2022 call the side effect over-stationarization. PatchTST's own ablation (Table 11) shows RevIN helps only slightly; patching and CI carry the gain.
Sub-series patching
Nie et al. 2023		Point-wise tokens are noisy and attention over L tokens is costlyGroups P steps into one token, so attention compares local shapes rather than single noisy readings; N ≈ L/S tokensA fixed patch length can straddle a step change, mixing "on" and "off" regimes inside one token (relevant to STEEL).
Channel independence
Nie et al. 2023; Han et al. 2024		Feature interference: one volatile channel corrupts shared embeddingsEach channel is a separate univariate series through a shared backboneDiscards cross-variable drivers such as temperature → load. Han et al. frame this as a capacity–robustness trade-off: CI is more robust to distribution drift but less expressive.
Self-attention distilling
Zhou et al. 2021		Memory growth across stacked encoder layersConv + ELU + max-pool with stride 2 between layers halves the sequence each time, keeping dominant featuresStabilizes memory, not predictions. Max-pooling favours extreme values, which may explain Informer's smoothed output on WTH and would amplify spikes on STEEL.

The argument the app has you practise: PatchTST's stability comes from reducing what attention has to see (few, locally meaningful, per-channel tokens) and normalizing each window, whereas Informer's distilling reduces cost without making the inputs better behaved. Neither mechanism handles discontinuous, near-constant industrial signals, which is where STEEL sits.

8Committee stress tests

Task 5 in your brief: five questions on whether PatchTST generalizes to non-stationary urban data. These seed the mock viva deck. Each opens to show what a strong answer contains and the follow-up the app will fire if you answer well.

Q1Household smart-meter load is spiky, sparse and full of near-zero periods, much like STEEL. Why did PatchTST succeed on London data and collapse on STEEL?

Strong answer contains

Whether the London series was aggregated (aggregation smooths spikes) or household-level; the difference in scale heterogeneity across features; the fact that household loads are rarely exactly constant, so the instance std stays away from zero; and an honest statement that the distinction is a hypothesis you can test with the diagnostics in section 5.

Follow-up

"If I gave you one household with a holiday home pattern, empty for two weeks, what happens to RevIN?"

Q2Urban demand shifts in regime: lockdowns, heatwaves, EV and heat-pump adoption. RevIN assumes the horizon looks like the lookback. How does PatchTST cope when it does not?

Strong answer contains

A precise statement of what RevIN corrects (level and scale shift between windows) and what it cannot (a change in shape or a structural break inside the horizon); the over-stationarization critique; and remedies such as rolling retraining, exogenous regime indicators, or evaluation on a deliberately shifted test period.

Follow-up

"Your chronological split puts all test data at the end. Was there any regime change in that period?"

Q3Channel independence ignores temperature when forecasting load, yet weather is the main driver of urban demand. Why is CI the right choice here?

Strong answer contains

The capacity–robustness trade-off (CI is more robust under drift and with heterogeneous channels, channel-mixing is more expressive); the empirical fact that CI won in your runs; the counter-designs iTransformer and Crossformer; and how you would test the question: add temperature as a covariate and compare.

Follow-up

"If CI discards cross-channel information, what exactly did Informer gain by mixing channels, and why did it underfit anyway?"

Q4You trained for 10 epochs on one chronological split, apparently with one seed. How confident are you that the ranking of models is stable?

Strong answer contains

Acknowledgement of the limitation; the size of the PatchTST margin relative to plausible seed variance (the PatchTST paper reports small seed variance on large datasets, Table 14); and the stronger protocol: rolling-origin evaluation, several seeds, and a Diebold–Mariano test for significance of forecast differences.

Follow-up

"Informer won on MAE at 5 epochs on WTH. Why should I believe the 10-epoch result is the stable one?"

Q5Your horizon is 24 steps with a 96-step lookback. The PatchTST paper evaluates horizons up to 720 with lookbacks of 336–512. Is this long-term forecasting, and would your conclusions hold at longer horizons on 6 GB of VRAM?

Strong answer contains

A clear position on what "long-term" means in your setup; the memory arithmetic showing that patching makes longer lookbacks affordable (L = 336, P = 16, S = 8 gives 41 tokens, still small); where Autoformer and Informer would run out of memory; and the DLinear finding that simple linear models are competitive at long horizons, which you should be ready to discuss because it was not among your baselines.

Follow-up

"Why was no linear baseline such as DLinear included, given that paper is in your bibliography?"

9Study mechanics

Mastery ladder

Every concept moves through four levels. Cards are generated for each level so that recognition is never mistaken for mastery.

Level 1Recognize

Pick the right answer from options. "Which component halves the sequence length between Informer encoder layers?"

Level 2Explain

Produce it from a blank prompt, in your own words, then compare to the model answer.

Level 3Defend

Answer an objection, with the thesis anchor and a source. Timed at 90 seconds.

Level 4Extend

Apply it to a new case: a different dataset, horizon, or hardware budget.

Card types

TypeExampleLevelsMode
Recall"State the three design choices that explain PatchTST's advantage."1–2Both
Derivation"L = 96, P = 16, S = 8. How many patches, and by what factor does the attention matrix shrink?" (11; 96² / 11² ≈ 76×)2–4Intensive
Figure readingThesis Fig 4.6 with the Informer line highlighted: "Why is this model's low MAE misleading?"1–3Both
Contrast"Distilling vs patching: both shorten sequences. What does each do to the information attention sees?"2–3Both
Objection"StandardScaler already equalizes feature variance. So how can variance disparity cause NaN?"3Intensive
Register"§3.5 says 60/20/20 and §4.1 says 70/20/10. Which is correct and what do you say if asked?"3Intensive
Transfer"A utility offers a Jetson with 4 GB. Which model do you deploy for 10,000 feeders and why?"4Intensive

Scheduling

Spaced repetition with a modified SM-2 schedule: after each review you rate your answer Again, Hard, Good or Easy, and the interval grows accordingly. Two changes suit a fixed defense date. Intervals are capped so that every card is seen at least twice in the final ten days. And each card's priority is weighted by its risk, so register items and STEEL cards surface before comfortable ones.

priority = (1 − retention) × (1 + risk weight) × days-to-defense factor

Readiness score

One number on the home screen: the share of concepts at level 3 or above, adjusted down for unresolved high-severity register items and for any pillar below 60%. It is honest by design; it will not reach 100% while a known inconsistency is unaddressed.
